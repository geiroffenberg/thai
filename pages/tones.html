The 5 Tone Shapes — See Them, Hear Them

Each line shows how your pitch moves. Match the bounce with your voice — Thai has 5 of them.

Mid  เสียงสามัญ samman — steady, level
Low  เสียงเอก ayk — drops and stays low
Falling  เสียงโท tho — starts high, drops hard
High  เสียงตรี tree — high and stays up
Rising  เสียงจัตวา chatawaa — climbs like a question

Tones Are Meaning

Same sound pattern, different tone = a totally different word. Get the tone wrong and Thais hear the wrong word.

Tip: หมา (rising) means dog, but ม้า (falling) means horse! One slip and you're asking to pat a horse instead of a dog.

Tone Marks You Will See

These tell your voice where to go — but read the rules below, because the mark alone doesn't always decide the tone.

How to Decide the Real Tone (The Part That Tricks Everyone)

A tone mark is only part of the answer. Follow 3 easy points to get the exact tone every time.

POINT 1

Is the consonant high, mid, or low class?

Every Thai consonant belongs to one of three "voice classes". This is the key.

POINT 2

Is the syllable live or dead?

Live = long vowel or a smooth final (m, n, ng). Dead = short vowel or a hard stop final (p, t, k).

POINT 3

Is there a tone mark?

No mark? Start from the first column of the table. Mark? Cross-reference your class column. That gives the tone.

High class ข ฉ ถ ผ ฝ ศ ษ ส ห

No mark on a live syllable → rising tone. No mark, dead syllable → low tone.

Mid class ก จ ด ต บ ป อ

No mark → mid tone, whether live or dead. The “safe” default for beginners.

Low class the rest (ค ง ช ญ ฯลฯ)

No mark live → mid tone. No mark dead → low tone. Most common class.

Ear Practice — same syllable, five tones

Say the same “ma” five ways by moving only your pitch. One syllable, five meanings, five shapes.

มาcome (mid)
หม่าgalangal (low)
ม้าhorse (falling)
ม๊าrhetorical (high)
หมาdog (rising)

Goal: shape the voice clearly, not perfect speed. Repeat until the rising “ma” (dog) vs falling “ma” (horse) feel obvious.
